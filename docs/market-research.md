# Market and compliance research behind the marketplace

Gathered 13 October 2026 with web searches. Where a source was a blog or a secondary summary, it is marked. Nothing here is legal advice, and several points need checking against primary law before launch.

## What the research says, and what we did about it

| Finding | Source | What Maxx does |
|---|---|---|
| EU Digital Services Act art. 30 makes marketplaces collect and make reasonable efforts to verify trader name, address, phone, email, ID, payment account, trade register number and a self-certification, and show it to consumers | [CCPC guide](https://www.ccpc.ie/enforcement-and-regulation/digital/the-digital-services-act), [Mondaq](https://mondaq.co.uk/compliance/1367256/online-marketplaces-and-the-digital-services-act-understanding-trader-traceability), [Freshfields](https://technologyquotient.freshfields.com/post/102lx12/dsa-decoded-9-the-dsa-and-online-marketplaces) | The seller application collects these fields and a four-part certification; buyers see trader details after buying. We do not verify documents (stated plainly in the product). Whether the small-business exemption applies to art. 30 was unclear in the sources and is flagged for counsel |
| US INFORM Consumers Act: sellers with 200+ sales and $5,000+ revenue in 12 months need verified ID and bank details, annual certification, and public disclosure above $20,000 | [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/new-inform-consumers-act-imposes-seller-diligence-and-disclosure-requirements-for-online-marketplaces/), [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2023/03/inform-consumers-act-imposes-new-requirements) | Same data model. | Groupon's regulator problems were refusing refunds, offering credit instead of money, slow complaints and inaccurate descriptions | [GOV.UK CMA release](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/groupon-commits-to-offer-refunds-and-improve-customer-service), [Lewis Silkin](https://adlaw.lewissilkin.com/post/102h4ul/groupon-gets-a-telling-off-from-the-cma) | Refunds are always cash to the original PayPal method, never credit. Hold money until the seller ships. Seller must answer in 24 hours or the buyer is refunded. Every report is recorded at once |
| The EU 14-day withdrawal right applies to consumer purchases from traders; platforms must communicate the position clearly | [iubenda](https://www.iubenda.com/en/blog/understanding-the-right-of-withdrawal-in-the-eu-a-guide-for-online-businesses/), [Sharetribe](https://www.sharetribe.com/academy/eu-right-of-withdrawal-button/) (vendor guides, not primary law) | Sellers must allow returns for at least 14 days; the consumer-rights page explains it. | EU AI Act art. 50: people must be told when they interact with an AI system, from 2 August 2026 | [secureprivacy](https://secureprivacy.ai/blog/eu-ai-act-article-50-transparency-obligations-for-chatbots-and-deepfakes-2026), [Law and More](https://lawandmore.eu/blog/is-your-chatbot-ready-for-the-eu-ai-act-a-compliance-checklist-for-dutch-businesses) (commentary; dates for marking duties conflicted) | Negotiation messages are labelled as agents, the hold dialog says AI negotiates, and a dedicated AI policy lists what is automated and how to get a human review |
| PayPal authorizations hold funds for 29 days with a 3-day honor period; partner marketplace flows need approved partner status and seller onboarding | [PayPal docs](https://developer.paypal.com/docs/checkout/standard/customize/authorization-honor/), [PayPal limited-release docs](https://developer.paypal.com/limited-release/commerce-platform/v3/payment/authorize-capture) | Hold copy says up to 29 days; production money flow is documented as needing PayPal marketplace approval |

Trust features (hold, pay-on-ship, accountable sellers) are the positioning |

## Gaps in the research

- No primary-source reading of the DSA, INFORM Act, Consumer Rights Directive or AI Act text; sources were law-firm alerts and vendor guides.
- Nothing on Pinduoduo or other group-buying fraud cases, or on PayPal marketplace fee and partner terms.
- No data on actual complaint rates in group buying beyond the Groupon actions.
- Country-by-country payment-services licensing was not researched. Collecting money and paying sellers later is the part most likely to need a licence or PayPal's marketplace product.

## Narrative decisions

The earlier line "See the price. Know your next move." described only the scan tool, which is the entry point, not the product. The product is group buying with a hard price limit. The site now leads with **Buy together. Pay less.** and explains in order: set a maximum, PayPal holds it, agents negotiate, pay only the winning price, the seller ships, and you are refunded if they do not. The scan page, seller pages, footer and every policy use the same story, and every page says when something is simulated.
